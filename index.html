Small tips. Better responses.

A useful prompt gives Copilot a clear outcome and enough detail to help. These four ingredients can improve your results—whether you are catching up on the week or exploring feedback.

GoalWhat outcome are you looking for?
ContextWho is it for, and why does it matter?
SourceWhat information should Copilot use?
ExpectationsWhat format, tone or detail do you need?

You do not need to include everything in one long prompt. Start with a focused request, check the response, then add context or ask a follow-up. Treat it as a conversation you can steer.

Your work firstCopilot Chat · optional Outlook5 interactive steps

1. Catch up on what matters Copilot Chat

Start a new work chat with Work IQ on where enabled. Add relevant work references with / or Add work content if you want to focus on a particular HR portfolio. Run the prompt as written, then check the date range and sources.

What may need my attention?

Help me close out the week. State the date range and time zone you use. Review my email, Teams discussions and meetings from this week. Surface five developments or requests I may need to address: what changed, the ask or decision, stated owner and due date, and source link. Distinguish explicit commitments from possible follow-ups. Exclude restricted personnel matters. Flag retrieval gaps; do not claim this is a complete inbox audit.

Your turn: open two cited threads and check their latest messages. Correct one finding or add a missing reference. “Not surfaced” does not prove that a message was read, ignored or never sent.

2. Find the loose ends Same chat

Separate pending from already resolved

Which asks appear unanswered in these sources, which decisions remain open, and which items were resolved by a later message? Show the latest supporting date and source. Separate requests to me from dependencies on others. Mark absent owners or deadlines Not specified. Do not infer someone ignored a message. Ask me one question about the highest-impact uncertainty, then wait.

Your turn: answer the question and confirm whether the apparent gap still exists. Check Outlook or Teams yourself if the latest reply is missing. Remove resolved items from the proposed follow-up list.

3. Decide what deserves action Same chat

Make an HR leadership action list

Using our verified findings, propose three priorities: address before closing the week, clarify with a partner, or carry into next week. Explain the impact on leader readiness, workforce plans or program delivery. Keep source links and distinguish stated deadlines from suggested checkpoints. Separate my actions from others' actions. Do not invent commitments, send messages or create tasks.

Your turn: change the priority of one item and explain why. Select one action you can responsibly take; confirm the recipient, audience and facts before drafting.

4. Prepare one follow-up Same chat

Stay in Copilot Chat. Replace the bracketed text with the verified decision you selected in step 3, and add its source thread as a work reference or permitted excerpt if needed. Use the conversation you have already built to create the follow-up email.

Prepare, review, then send

Send a concise follow-up email in Outlook about [decision to follow up on], using our verified findings and its source context. Address the decision and clarify the next step. If my intended action is unclear, ask me one question before creating the response. Use a clear, collaborative senior HR tone. Distinguish proposed actions or dates from agreed commitments. Invent no facts or agreement; omit sensitive personnel details.

Your turn: compare the response with the source, correct any missing context and edit its tone. Decide what you would change before using it in a real communication.

5. Create your weekly closeout brief Same chat

Stay in your original Copilot Chat conversation. Use the verified findings and selected priorities to bring the week's information together.

Keep the signal for next week

Create a weekly HR closeout brief using only the findings I verified and the priorities I selected in this chat.

Lead with the three issues that most need my attention. For each, state the business or people-program impact, the confirmed ask, the stated owner and due date, and the source. Separate resolved items, actions I accepted, dependencies on partners and proposed checkpoints for next week. Preserve Not specified and Needs validation labels wherever facts remain unclear.

End with two questions I should revisit next week and a short list of source gaps. Do not imply that you searched every message or know what I read. Do not send, schedule, create tasks or make commitments for anyone.

Create an interactive visual to help me explore this information.

Your turn: check the brief and interactive visual against your verified findings. Correct unsupported claims and confirm that proposed next steps are not presented as agreed commitments.

Let the thread supply the context

Create a concise reply addressing the latest unresolved request or decision in this email thread, using only the thread's context. Infer what needs a response from the messages and clarify the next step. If my intended action is unclear, ask me one question before creating the reply. Use a clear, collaborative senior HR tone. Distinguish proposed actions or dates from agreed commitments. Invent no facts or agreement; omit sensitive personnel details.

Your turn: compare the draft with the latest thread, edit its tone and check recipients. Leave it unsent during the exercise. Sending any real communication is a separate human decision.

Love your results?

Create a scheduled prompt to generate a fresh weekly closeout brief automatically, ready for your review.

  1. Make it reusable. In Copilot Chat with Work IQ on, adapt your brief prompt to review each week's email, Teams discussions and meetings. Include the desired format, source links and uncertainty labels; remove references to findings you verified in this chat. Run it once and review the result.
  2. Schedule it. Hover over your reusable prompt and select Schedule this prompt.
  3. Choose your weekly rhythm. Set a weekly repeat, your preferred day and time, and the number of runs. Check the time zone and choose whether to receive an email notification.
  4. Save and review. Select Save. Open each new result under Chats and verify its findings before acting.

Manage or turn off runs under Settings and more (…) → Scheduled prompts. Scheduling help.
