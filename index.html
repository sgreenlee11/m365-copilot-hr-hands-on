01 / REFLECT · 15 MINUTES

Tell your
impact story.

Draft your quarterly self-review. Let the evidence do the talking; keep the judgment and voice your own.

Core: Copilot ChatAdvanced: evidence audit / CoworkOutput: first-person self-review
Your practice file

Six months of context. One quarter to review. Deliberate evidence gaps.

Download TXT ↓
  1. Download → attach or paste. Start a new chat. Provide the complete self-review evidence file, including the review period and source IDs.
  2. Run → refine. Copy the core prompt below. Read the evidence map before accepting the draft.
  3. Verify → personalize. Check each claim against the IDs. Replace generic phrasing with your own voice; do not add unverified results.

Core prompt / evidence before eloquence

I need to draft my Q3 2026 self-review for Northstar Retail, covering July 1 through September 30, so I can discuss my contributions and growth with my manager.

Use only the supplied synthetic self-review evidence file. April-June entries are background, not Q3 accomplishments. This is my first-person self-review, not a manager's assessment or an employee rating.

First build a compact evidence map: contribution, date, my role, supported outcome, source ID, and limitation. Separate my work from team outcomes. Reconcile conflicting figures; do not choose a convenient number. Flag missing evidence as Needs validation.

Then draft 350-450 words with: highest-impact contributions, collaboration, a challenge and what I learned, and two proposed next-quarter goals. Use a balanced, specific first-person voice. Quantify only supported outcomes; do not imply causation or invent financial savings. Cite source IDs next to factual claims. Keep unresolved items in a separate validation list, not disguised as achievements. Do not submit, save to an HR system, or share anything.

Challenge prompt / test the story

Audit this draft against the supplied evidence. Identify out-of-period credit, double counting, ambiguous attribution, unsupported causation and conflicting metrics. For each issue, show the claim, source IDs and a safer rewrite. Distinguish a development goal from an achieved result. Revise the self-review only after showing the audit.
Advanced / Cowork starting prompt

Only after the facilitator confirms GA feature access and budget. Provide the same source file first. Otherwise run each stage in Copilot Chat.

Build a review-ready package

I need a review-ready Q3 2026 self-review package for my own reflection at Northstar Retail.

1. Bound the quarter. Read only the supplied synthetic evidence file. Confirm July 1-September 30 as the review period and isolate April-June context. Name the sources you can actually read; stop and request the file if missing.
2. Reconcile my contribution. Build an evidence ledger with source IDs, dates, my contribution versus team contribution, supported outcomes, conflicting metrics and missing proof. Do not invent facts or quantify unsupported benefits.
3. Build the package in this conversation. Draft a 350-450 word first-person self-review, a claim-to-evidence table and a short manager-discussion agenda. Include learning and two proposed next-quarter goals, clearly separate from achievements.
4. Quality-check. Audit period boundaries, attribution and numbers. List unresolved questions and the evidence I need to answer them.

Keep Needs validation labels. This is not an employee rating or employment decision. Prepare text only. Do not save, send, share, submit or write back without my separate explicit approval for each action.

What good looks like

  • R01 is prior-quarter context, not a Q3 achievement.
  • R03's 120 and R04's 112 are not interchangeable: use 112 verified completions, disclose the discrepancy.
  • R05 supports a team-level trend, not a causal claim or invented savings.
  • R07's pilot has no proven outcome yet; a proposed goal remains a proposal.

Reflect: Which sentence became more credible when you reduced its certainty?

02 / DESIGN · 20 MINUTES

Make leadership
a practice.

Design a leadership effectiveness session for a senior team. Move from broad themes to a timed, usable facilitation plan.

Core: Copilot ChatAdvanced: design critique / CoworkOutput: 90-minute facilitation plan
Your practice file

Team brief, learning notes and operational constraints. All fictional.

Download TXT ↓
  1. Download → attach or paste. Start a new chat and provide the leadership session file. These are fictional internal materials, not a real SharePoint source.
  2. Run → inspect the flow. Check that the proposed activities serve trust, decision-making and ways of working, rather than just filling time.
  3. Verify → adapt. Add the minutes. Check the room constraints, voluntary participation and the follow-through owner.

Core prompt / design for a useful outcome

I need a 90-minute leadership effectiveness session for the eight-person fictional Northstar Retail senior team, focused on trust, decision-making and ways of working.

Use only the supplied synthetic team brief and learning notes L01-L05. Cite those IDs for constraints and design choices. Do not claim access to an internal learning portal or SharePoint. Separate fictional internal guidance from any external framework. No web research is needed.

Produce: three observable learning objectives; a timed agenda totaling exactly 90 minutes including opening, transitions and closing; and 3-4 activities with purpose, instructions, facilitator questions, materials, timing and a tangible output. Include a voluntary, non-diagnostic opening pulse and an exit check; do not represent either as a validated assessment.

Offer a safe way to pass or contribute silently. Avoid forced personal disclosure, psychometric scoring or clinical diagnosis. Include a simple decision practice and a working-agreement exercise. End with a 30-day follow-through plan with proposed roles (not invented commitments), evidence of progress, and unresolved questions. Mark assumptions and explain why each activity fits the brief. Draft only; do not invite participants or publish materials.

Challenge prompt / make the session hold up

Stress-test the plan: do all minutes, including transitions, total 90? Are there exactly 3-4 activities? Where might a quieter participant be excluded or a power imbalance affect candor? Show one alternative to public disclosure. Check each design choice against L01-L05, then revise the weakest activity. Do not call the pulse a validated diagnostic.
Advanced / Cowork starting prompt

Only with facilitator-confirmed GA access and usage budget; otherwise complete the stages in Copilot Chat.

Build a facilitator-ready package

I need a facilitator-ready leadership effectiveness session for eight senior leaders at fictional Northstar Retail.

1. Read the brief. Use only supplied L01-L05. Confirm purpose, audience, 90-minute limit, materials and safety boundaries. Ask for missing source content rather than pretending to access internal systems.
2. Design the experience. Propose three observable objectives and 3-4 activities addressing trust, decision-making and ways of working. Include a voluntary, non-diagnostic pulse, silent contribution and a right to pass.
3. Draft the package here. Provide a timed 90-minute agenda including transitions, a facilitator run sheet, participant instructions, working-agreement template and a proposed 30-day follow-through tracker. For each activity specify purpose, minutes, materials, questions and output. Cite source IDs.
4. Audit the design. Sum the timings, check inclusion and power dynamics, and list assumptions and tradeoffs. Keep fictional internal guidance distinct from external frameworks. No psychometric or clinical interpretation.

Use text in this conversation. Do not create calendar events, save files, send invitations, publish or share without my separate explicit approval for each action. Do not invent agreed owners or commitments.

What good looks like

  • The full agenda totals 90 minutes, not 90 plus breaks.
  • Three or four activities produce concrete outputs, not just discussion.
  • The pulse measures participants' stated perceptions, not personality or psychological health.
  • Decisions have a proposed owner and revisit point; participants confirm their own commitments.

Reflect: What would you remove so the team has time to make one real agreement?

03 / REHEARSE · 15 MINUTES

Practice
the hard part.

A strong contributor. Slipped delivery. Prepare honest feedback without assuming the reason, then rehearse one turn at a time.

Core: Copilot ChatAdvanced: adaptive rehearsalOutput: opening + practice + debrief
Your practice file

A hypothetical delivery conversation with observable facts and unknowns.

Download TXT ↓
  1. Download → attach or paste. Start a new chat. Provide the conversation brief. Keep the role-play fictional; do not enter real personnel information.
  2. Prepare → rehearse. Run the preparation prompt, review it, then run the rehearsal prompt. You play the leader; Copilot plays the contributor.
  3. Pause → debrief. After 3-5 exchanges, type DEBRIEF. Compare the feedback with the facts, not with a guessed motive.

Core prompt / prepare with empathy and clarity

I need to prepare for a hypothetical conversation with Morgan, a strong contributor whose recent delivery has slipped at fictional Northstar Retail.

Use only the supplied synthetic conversation brief C01-C05. Separate observed facts from unknown causes. Do not infer motives, medical conditions, protected traits or personal circumstances.

Recommend an honest, empathetic approach. Draft a 60-90 second opening using Situation-Behavior-Impact: a specific situation, observable behavior and supported work impact, followed by an open question. Cite source IDs in the preparation notes, not as unnatural spoken dialogue. Acknowledge strengths without weakening the delivery concern.

Suggest three possible reactions as hypothetical, not predictions about Morgan, and a respectful response to each. Include questions to understand barriers, support options within the brief, and a proposed mutual delivery checkpoint. Do not invent agreement or escalate to disciplinary action. This is preparation, not an automated employment decision. Do not send or record anything in a personnel system.

Rehearsal prompt / one turn at a time

Let's rehearse using the same fictional brief. I am the leader; you play Morgan. Begin by asking me to give my opening, then STOP and wait for my reply.

After each of my messages, respond only as Morgan in 1-3 sentences and wait. Do not write my lines, give coaching during the role-play or run the entire conversation at once. Use only the brief; if a detail is unknown, say so or label a new role-play detail hypothetical. Do not add medical or personal explanations.

If I type PAUSE, stop role-playing and help me rephrase. If I type DEBRIEF, end the role-play and coach me on specificity, empathy, listening and mutual next steps. Quote examples from my actual words, identify one improvement and give a revised opening. Do not score my suitability for employment or make personnel recommendations.
Advanced / introduce a realistic challenge

Practice listening, not winning

Restart the role-play. Use one hypothetical challenge: Morgan disagrees about the impact or asks for clearer priorities. Do not invent new historical facts. Wait for my opening, then respond one turn at a time. Keep the goal a shared understanding and a realistic checkpoint, not winning an argument. When I type DEBRIEF, compare my original and revised approaches using my actual words and C01-C05. State what still needs to be asked rather than inferred.
Best tool for this moment: stay in an interactive chat. Multi-step delegation is useful for preparing a package; a rehearsal needs the human to speak and the assistant to wait. No additional usage-based product is required here.

What good looks like

  • The opening uses specific missed dates and their documented impact.
  • A possible reaction is labeled hypothetical, never treated as evidence.
  • The assistant waits after one turn and honors PAUSE and DEBRIEF.
  • Support and checkpoints are proposals until both people agree.

Reflect: Did your question make room to understand, or quietly assume the answer?

04 / TRANSFER · 5 MINUTES

Better prepared.
Still accountable.

Copilot helps you get to a useful draft. You decide what is true, fair and appropriate to act on.

VERIFY

Trace the claim.

Check dates, numbers, attribution and evidence gaps. Confidence is not proof.

PROTECT

Respect the boundary.

Use approved tools and permitted data. Training prompts are not permission to process personnel records.

OWN

Make the decision.

Review the words and next steps yourself. Never delegate an employment decision.

Your next move

Pick one workflow you can safely improve. Write its goal, audience, approved sources, output and review gate. Start with synthetic content; move to work data only under your organization's policies.

Pair reflection: What changed the quality of your thinking, not just the speed of drafting? Share the learning, not personal or personnel content.

Facilitator notes & product sources

Suggested pacing: 5-minute setup, three labs (15 / 20 / 15 minutes), 5-minute reflection. Core prompts support beginners; challenge prompts add depth without requiring additional products. All outputs are variable, so use the verification checklists rather than expecting an identical model answer.

Preflight before every delivery: verify current GA status, participants' licenses, work-account access, file-input policy, enabled Cowork features and approved consumption limits. Test with the exact attendee experience. Skip a route if any gate fails. No preview functionality is part of this lab.
